## Executive Summary

Law 3 in [`docs/four-laws.md`](../../docs/four-laws.md#L60-L77) identifies a **real, provable mathematical obstruction** in adversarial transport, but its current formulation conflates three distinct things: (i) an overly broad informal claim that scalar critic potentials cannot encode geometric scale/mass information, (ii) genuine **operator-specific and information-theoretic non-identifiability** of *1-Lipschitz* ($W_1$) potentials paired with *fixed global-horizon* local transport operators, and (iii) an overstated claim that RGDT's `t3_factorial_ablation` proves a "clean $3 \times 3$ triple dissociation" of three mathematically independent mechanisms.

Our adversarial mathematical, forensic code/receipt, and generator-free (`G = Id`) provably globally 1-Lipschitz GPU audit establishes six main conclusions:

1. **Naive "scalar potentials are insufficient" overclaim is FALSIFIED — the true obstruction is 1-Lipschitz ($W_1$) ray-length non-identifiability + fixed local horizon:** Under quadratic $W_2$ transport, a scalar Brenier–Kantorovich potential $\varphi$ **uniquely determines** the optimal map $T^\star(x) = x - \nabla\varphi(x)$ and recovers an $8\times$ mode-width ratio in one step (`rel_std_err = 0.0013`), precisely because $\lVert\nabla\varphi(x)\rVert = \lVert x - T^\star(x)\rVert$ violates the 1-Lipschitz bound (`sup |grad phi| = 2.01 / 2.27 > 1`). Conversely, under **globally 1-Lipschitz** ($W_1$) Kantorovich potentials, transport rays determine direction ($\lVert\nabla D^\star\rVert = 1$) while leaving transport distance, local scale, and mass split **mathematically non-identifiable** (Ambrosio 2003; Evans–Gangbo 1999; Santambrogio 2015). We construct explicit target pairs $(P_1, P_2)$ achieving exact LP duality gap $\le 1.11 \times 10^{-16}$ under the **identical** global zero-mean-gauged 1-Lipschitz potential $D^\star$ for both $8\times$ scale/mass mixtures (**Theorem 1**) and $42.4\sigma$ orthogonal saddle shifts (**Theorem 2**). Even more sharply, dynamically re-solving the **global** $W_1$ LP dual per batch paired with per-particle adaptive Adam (`w1_analytic_adam_lr020`, $\sup \lVert\nabla D\rVert_2 \le 0.999977$) **passes** `t3_iso_scale_mass_8x` (`core_min_eigen_ratio = 0.6778`, `core_cov = 0.2125`), and dynamically re-solved batch $W_1$ LP SGD (`batch_w1_lp_1lip_sgd`, $\sup \lVert\nabla D\rVert_2 \le 0.999961$) **passes** `t3_iso_horizon_saddle_shift` (`4/4` modes, `hq = 0.9766`) — proving that the obstruction is strictly **operator-specific** (fixed-horizon local / shared-step transport from static or local 1-Lipschitz potentials), not a blanket impossibility for every 1-Lipschitz critic coupled to a non-local LP or private per-particle state.

### Complete 8-Point Rubric Evaluations (`L3-C1` through `L3-C10`)

- **L3-C1 (`Falsified claim`) — Naive "Scalar Potentials Cannot Encode Local Scale/Geometry":**
  1. *Precise formulation:* The unqualified reading of [`docs/four-laws.md:63`](../../docs/four-laws.md#L63) that a scalar critic potential $D: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ cannot identify multi-scale local covariance or mass ratios.
  2. *Assumptions:* Tests both unconstrained smooth scalar potentials (Brenier $W_2$) and globally 1-Lipschitz dynamically re-solved finite-batch $W_1$ LP potentials paired with adaptive per-particle optimizers on `G = Id`.
  3. *Existing supporting evidence:* Prior Forge baselines (`bcap-pure-v1`, `empf-v8`) failed `t3_iso_scale_mass_8x` (`core_min_eigen_ratio = 0.0984`, `0.0680`), which only tested *neural* fixed-architecture critics with gradient penalties and shared optimizers.
  4. *Potential counterexamples:* (a) Exact $W_2$ Brenier scalar potential $\varphi(x)$ uniquely encodes arbitrary $8\times$ Gaussian/uniform width transforms via $T^\star(x) = x - \nabla\varphi(x)$; (b) dynamically re-solved exact batch $W_1$ LP dual ($\sup\lVert\nabla D\rVert_2 \le 0.999977$) + per-particle Adam (`betas=(0, 0.999)`) passes `t3_iso_scale_mass_8x`.
  5. *Minimal falsification experiment:* `verify_theorem1_and_brenier_refutation()` + `run_analytic_1lip_scale_mass_8x()` arm `w1_analytic_adam_lr020` in [`p2_law3_analytic_lipschitz.py`](../../scripts/four_laws_audit/p2_law3_analytic_lipschitz.py#L287-L348).
  6. *Relevant prior research:* Brenier (1991) polar factorization; McCann (1995) generalized Brenier theorem; Villani (2009), *Optimal Transport: Old and New*, Ch. 9–10.
  7. *Computational verification:* Brenier $W_2$ achieves `rel_std_err = 0.001300` on both $\sigma = 0.04$ and $\sigma = 0.32$ (`sup |grad phi| = 2.0100` / `2.2700 > 1`); `w1_analytic_adam_lr020` passes `t3_iso_scale_mass_8x` (`4/4` modes, `mass_tv = 0.0041`, `hq = 0.9434`, `core_cov = 0.2125`, `core_eig_min = 0.6778`).

- **L3-C2 (`Information-theoretic obstruction`) — Global 1-Lipschitz ($W_1$) Potential Non-Identifiability of Scale, Mass & Step Length (Theorem 1):**
  1. *Precise formulation:* For a source $\mu$ and two distinct targets $P_1 \neq P_2$ differing in $8\times$ component width (Pair 1A) or $2\times$ transport distance / $2\times$ moved mass (Pair 1B), the exact global zero-mean-gauged $`W_1`$-optimal 1-Lipschitz Kantorovich potential $D^\star$ and its gradient $\nabla D^\star$ are **mathematically identical everywhere on $`\mathbb{R}^d`$**; hence any memoryless local operator observing only $(x, D^\star(x), \nabla D^\star(x), \nabla^2 D^\star(x))$ has zero mutual information about which of $(P_1, P_2)$ is the target.
  2. *Assumptions:* Exact population $W_1$ Kantorovich dual ($\mathrm{Lip}(D) \le 1$, zero-mean gauge $`\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}_\mu[D] + \mathbb{E}_P[D]) = 0`$); operator observes only local potential jet at $x$ without non-local target sample coupling or multi-step adaptive memory.
  3. *Existing supporting evidence:* Empirical failure of `bcap-pure-v1`, `empf-v8`, `rhbi-v12` on `t3_iso_scale_mass_8x` ([`readout-18e9fbb0`](../forge/records/readout-18e9fbb07a4d8c24ba8e924b.json), [`readout-7e0c3159`](../forge/records/readout-7e0c315962234ea1c2c2ba3f.json), [`readout-8a63e947`](../forge/records/readout-8a63e947313fcb7d2e62a8d2.json)).
  4. *Potential counterexamples:* Broken if (i) the 1-Lipschitz constraint is lifted (e.g., $W_2$ Brenier potential), (ii) the critic is dynamically re-optimized on finite batches *and* paired with per-particle adaptive step memory (`w1_analytic_adam_lr020`), or (iii) the operator directly inspects $`Y_{\text{real}}`$ pairwise geometry (conformal horizon / $`k`$-NN density ratio).
  5. *Minimal falsification experiment:* `verify_theorem1_and_brenier_refutation()` + fixed-step SGD arms `w1_analytic_sgd_lr{005,020,050}` in `run_analytic_1lip_scale_mass_8x()`.
  6. *Relevant prior research:* Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality; Evans & Gangbo (1999) Monge transport rays; Ambrosio (2003); Santambrogio (2015), *Optimal Transport for Applied Mathematicians*, Ch. 3 (transport density $\sigma$ vs Kantorovich potential $u$); Gulrajani et al. (2017) WGAN-GP; Stanczuk et al. (2021) "Wasserstein GANs Work Because They Fail (to Approximate the Wasserstein Distance)".

- **L3-C3 (`Information-theoretic obstruction`) — Beyond-Horizon & Equidistant Orthogonal Saddle-Shift Non-Identifiability (Theorem 2):**
  1. *Precise formulation:* In $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge 3$), for pre-shift $`\mu = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{ae_1} + \delta_{-ae_1})`$ and orthogonal post-shift targets $`P_v = \frac{1}{4}(\delta_{ae_1} + \delta_{-ae_1} + \delta_{av} + \delta_{-av})`$ ($v \in S^{d-2} \perp e_1$), (a) the **single** global zero-mean-gauged 1-Lipschitz potential $D^\star(x) = \min(\lVert x - a e_1\rVert, \lVert x + a e_1\rVert) - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}a$ is simultaneously $`W_1`$-optimal for **every** $v \perp e_1$, carrying $0$ bits about $v$; (b) even the target-dependent smooth symmetric potential $`D^{\text{sym}}_v`$ has exact zero orthogonal gradient $`\nabla_\perp D^{\text{sym}}_v(t e_1) = 0`$ on the entire $`e_1`$-axis; and (c) fixed-horizon screened Sinkhorn with screening cutoff $`c_{\text{screen}}`$ ($1.5\rho$ in `metric.py`, $\rho$ or $2\rho(\sqrt{e}-1)$ in `primitive.py`) and $`r_{\text{screen}} = \ell\sqrt{(c_{\text{screen}}+1)^2 - 1} \ll \sqrt{2}\,a`$ assigns zero active transport mass to all orphan samples.
  2. *Assumptions:* Exact $W_1$ 1-Lipschitz dual or fixed-horizon $(\ell, \rho)$ Born–Infeld screening without non-local orphan coupling or dynamically re-solved global finite-batch LP.

## 3. Formal Observation Model & Non-Identifiability Theorems

To replace informal talk of "scalar potentials are insufficient" with rigorous mathematics, we define the exact observation channel of a transport operator $\mathcal{T}$ acting on a current particle/pushforward measure $`\mu \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)`$ against an unknown target $`P \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)`$.

### 3.1 Formal Observation Channels

Let $`\mathcal{F}_1 := \{D : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R} \mid |D(x) - D(y)| \le \lVert x - y\rVert_2\ \forall x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d\}`$ denote the globally 1-Lipschitz potentials, and let $`\mathcal{D}^\star_1(\mu, P) := \arg\max_{D \in \mathcal{F}_1,\ \frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}_\mu D + \mathbb{E}_P D) = 0} (\mathbb{E}_P[D] - \mathbb{E}_\mu[D])`$ be the exact zero-mean-gauged $W_1$ Kantorovich dual maximizers. Define three nested observation channels at a query particle $x \in \mathrm{supp}(\mu)$:

1. **Static Global 1-Lipschitz Potential Jet Channel $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{1-Lip}}(\mu, P)`$:**
   For a potential $D^\star \in \mathcal{D}^\star_1(\mu, P)$, the transport operator observes the entire function $D^\star : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ (hence all values $D^\star(x)$, gradients $\nabla D^\star(x)$, Hessians $\nabla^2 D^\star(x)$, and higher derivatives wherever smooth), **plus** $\mu$, **without** observing raw target samples $Y \sim P$, target mass splits, or per-mode variances $\sigma_m^2$.
2. **Fixed-Horizon Local Screened Channel $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{loc}}(\mu, P; \ell, \rho)`$:**
   Augments $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{1-Lip}}`$ with pairwise target distances only inside the Born–Infeld screening ball $`B(x, r_{\text{screen}})`$, where $`\hat{c}(x, y; \ell) := \sqrt{1 + \lVert x - y\rVert_2^2/\ell^2} - 1 \le c_{\text{screen}}`$, i.e., $`r_{\text{screen}}(\ell, c_{\text{screen}}) = \ell \sqrt{(c_{\text{screen}} + 1)^2 - 1}`$ (at $\rho = 3.0$: $`c_{\text{screen}} = \rho = 3.0 \implies \sqrt{15}\,\ell = 3.8730\,\ell`$ in linear `primitive.py`; $`c_{\text{screen}} = 2\rho(\sqrt{e}-1) = 3.8923 \implies 4.7890\,\ell`$ in rapidity `primitive.py`; and $`c_{\text{screen}} = 1.5\rho = 4.50 \implies \sqrt{29.25}\,\ell = 5.4083\,\ell`$ in `metric.py`'s shifted `sinkhorn_slack`), for a single global scalar horizon $`\ell > 0`$ (e.g., 25th-percentile target NN distance) and without memory of past particle trajectories or a global $O(B^3)$ LP coupling solver.
3. **Unresidualized Local Conditional Kernel Channel $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{cond}}(\mu, P; h_c, B, T)`$:**
   For conditional distributions $P(Y \mid c, t)$ stratified over $T$ discrete strata with minibatch size $B$, the operator observes $n_t \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(B, 1/T)$ samples per step and evaluates local kernel averages $\sum_j w_j(c_0) \psi(X_i, Y_j)$ with context bandwidth $`h_c > 0`$ directly on unresidualized coordinates $(X, Y)$.

**Definition (Operator Non-Identifiability):** An observation channel $\mathcal{O}$ is *non-identifiable* for a transport task $\mathcal{A}$ if there exist a source $\mu$ and two distinct target distributions $P_1 \neq P_2$ with disjoint optimal transport actions $\mathcal{A}(\mu, P_1) \cap \mathcal{A}(\mu, P_2) = \emptyset$ such that $\mathcal{O}(\mu, P_1) = \mathcal{O}(\mu, P_2)$ (identical potential functions / identical local observations). By the data-processing inequality, **no deterministic or randomized transport operator $\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{O})$ can simultaneously succeed on both $P_1$ and $`P_2`$** (minimax error $`\ge \frac{1}{2} d(\mathcal{A}(\mu, P_1), \mathcal{A}(\mu, P_2)) > 0`$).

### 3.2 Refutation of the Naive Overclaim: Exact $W_2$ Brenier Scalar Potentials

Before proving $W_1$ non-identifiability, we refute the overly broad statement that "a scalar potential $D(x)$ cannot encode local scale or covariance":

> **Proposition 0 (Brenier–McCann Scalar Sufficiency under Quadratic $W_2$):**
> Let $`\mu \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)`$ be absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure and let $`P \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)`$ be any finite-second-moment target. By Brenier's theorem (1991), there exists a $`\mu`$-a.e. unique convex scalar potential $\psi : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ — equivalently, a $`W_2`$-Kantorovich scalar potential $\varphi(x) := \frac{1}{2}\lVert x\rVert_2^2 - \psi(x)$ — such that the single-step gradient map $T^\star(x) := \nabla\psi(x) = x - \nabla\varphi(x)$ satisfies $`T^\star_\# \mu = P`$ and uniquely minimizes $`\mathbb{E}_\mu[\lVert x - T(x)\rVert_2^2]`$.

**Concrete witness (`verify_theorem1_and_brenier_refutation()`):** For $\mu = \mathrm{Unif}[-1.05, -0.95]$ ($`\sigma_{\text{src}} = 0.05/\sqrt{3}`$) pushing onto $\mathrm{Unif}[1 - \sigma_k, 1 + \sigma_k]$ with $\sigma_1 = 0.04$ (narrow) vs $\sigma_2 = 0.32$ ($8\times$ wide), the quadratic scalar potential $\varphi_k(x) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \frac{\sigma_k}{0.05})(x + 1)^2 - 2(x + 1) + c_k$ produces $T_k(x) = x - \varphi_k'(x) = 1 + \frac{\sigma_k}{0.05}(x + 1)$, recovering exact target widths in **one step** (`narrow_0.04`: `pushed_std = 0.023124` vs `true_std = 0.023094`, `rel_std_err = 0.001300`; `wide_0.32`: `pushed_std = 0.184992` vs `true_std = 0.184752`, `rel_std_err = 0.001300`). Crucially, $\varphi_k$ succeeds **only because it violates Law 2's 1-Lipschitz bound**: $`\sup_x |\varphi_1'(x)| = 2.0100 > 1`$ and $`\sup_x |\varphi_2'(x)| = 2.2700 > 1`$ (since $|\varphi'(x)| = |x - T^\star(x)|$ encodes exact transport distance in the gradient magnitude!).

**OT-theoretic root cause (Ambrosio 2003; Evans & Gangbo 1999; Santambrogio 2015, Ch. 3):** Under $W_2$ ($c(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\lVert x - y\rVert^2$), the optimal Kantorovich potential $\varphi$ satisfies $\nabla\varphi(x) = x - T^\star(x)$, so its gradient norm **equals the transport distance** and its Hessian $`\nabla^2\varphi = I - J_{T^\star}`$ encodes exact local stretch. Under $W_1$ ($c(x, y) = \lVert x - y\rVert_2$), duality imposes the global constraint $\lVert\nabla D(x)\rVert_2 \le 1$, with $\lVert\nabla D(x)\rVert_2 = 1$ identically along every non-trivial transport ray $[x, T(x)]$. Thus $\nabla D^\star(x) = (T(x) - x)/\lVert T(x) - x\rVert_2$ encodes **only the unit ray direction**; the transport *distance*, *local Jacobian stretch*, and *transport density* $\sigma$ solving the Beckmann PDE $-\nabla \cdot (\sigma \nabla D^\star) = P - \mu$ are completely decoupled from $D^\star$ itself.

### 3.3 Theorem 1 — Global $W_1$ Non-Identifiability of Local Scale, Mode Mass, and Transport Distance

> **Theorem 1 (Scale, Mass, and Step-Length Non-Identifiability in $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{1-Lip}}`$):**
> Let $d \ge 1$, let $`\rho_\perp \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})`$ be any transverse measure (trivial for $d = 1$), and define the source $`\mu = \mathrm{Unif}[-1.05, -0.95] \otimes \rho_\perp`$. Consider three strictly distinct target measures:
> - $`P_{1a} = \big(\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Unif}[-1.05, -0.95] + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Unif}[0.96, 1.04]\big) \otimes \rho_\perp`$ (narrow right mode, half-width $0.04$),
> - $`P_{2a} = \big(\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Unif}[-1.05, -0.95] + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Unif}[0.68, 1.32]\big) \otimes \rho_\perp`$ ($8\times$ wider right mode, half-width $0.32$),
> - $`P_{2b} = \big(\tfrac{3}{4}\mathrm{Unif}[-1.05, -0.95] + \tfrac{1}{4}\mathrm{Unif}[2.68, 3.32]\big) \otimes \rho_\perp`$ ($2\times$ less moved mass, $2\times$ longer transport distance, $8\times$ wider mode).
>
> Then **all three pairs** $`(\mu, P_{1a})`$, $`(\mu, P_{2a})`$, and $`(\mu, P_{2b})`$ share:
> 1. The exact same Wasserstein-1 distance $W_1(\mu, P) = 1.0$,
> 2. The **exact same globally 1-Lipschitz, zero-mean-gauged optimal Kantorovich potential** $D^\star(x) = x_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}$ everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^d$ (hence identical values, constant gradient $\nabla D^\star(x) \equiv e_1$, and zero higher derivatives everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^d$).
>
> Consequently, under $`\mathcal{O}_{\text{1-Lip}}`$, $`P_{1a}`$, $`P_{2a}`$, and $`P_{2b}`$ are **100% observationally indistinguishable**, yet require mutually incompatible transport actions (preserving $`50\%`$ vs $`75\%`$ of source mass, moving the remainder distance $2$ vs $4$, and scaling local width by $0.8\times$ vs $6.4\times$).

*Proof.* First, $D^\star(x) = x_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}$ has $\lVert\nabla D^\star(x)\rVert_2 \equiv 1$, so $`D^\star \in \mathcal{F}_1`$. Second, $`\mathbb{E}_\mu[x_1] = -1`$, while $`\mathbb{E}_{P_{1a}}[x_1] = \mathbb{E}_{P_{2a}}[x_1] = \tfrac{1}{2}(-1) + \tfrac{1}{2}(1) = 0`$ and $`\mathbb{E}_{P_{2b}}[x_1] = \tfrac{3}{4}(-1) + \tfrac{1}{4}(3) = 0`$. Therefore:
- Zero-mean gauge holds identically for all three: $`\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}_\mu[D^\star] + \mathbb{E}_P[D^\star]) = \tfrac{1}{2}((-1 + \tfrac{1}{2}) + (0 + \tfrac{1}{2})) = 0`$.
- Dual objective is $`\mathbb{E}_P[D^\star] - \mathbb{E}_\mu[D^\star] = \tfrac{1}{2} - (-\tfrac{1}{2}) = 1.0`$ for all three.
- For each $`P \in \{P_{1a}, P_{2a}, P_{2b}\}`$, consider the coupling $\pi$ that keeps the common mass ($0.5$ for $`P_{1a}, P_{2a}`$; $0.75$ for $`P_{2b}`$) fixed on $[-1.05, -0.95]$ and couples the remaining mass monotonically to the right mode (strictly to the right of $-0.95$, since $`0.68 > -0.95`$ and $`2.68 > -0.95`$) with identical transverse coordinate $`x_\perp = y_\perp`$. Integrating against $\pi$ yields $`\int \lVert y - x\rVert_2\,d\pi = \mathbb{E}_P[D^\star] - \mathbb{E}_\mu[D^\star] = 1.0 = W_1(\mu, P)`$, proving $D^\star \in \mathcal{D}^\star_1(\mu, P)$ simultaneously for all three targets. $\square$

## 4. Mechanism Independence Audit & Generalized Midpoint Cancellation Proof

### 4.1 Are the Three Law-3 Mechanisms Independent? (Two Halves, Not Three Orthogonal Pillars)

Combining our theoretical theorems (Section 3) and verbatim factorial receipts (Section 2.2) yields a clear structural answer: **the three Law-3 mechanisms are NOT mathematically independent.** Rather, Law 3 separates into **two mathematically distinct obstructions**:

1. **Law 3A — Spatial Metric & Non-Local Coupling Non-Identifiability (Unifying Pillars 1 & 2):**
   Both multi-scale covariance collapse (Pillar 1) and beyond-horizon orphan stranding (Pillar 2) stem from **the exact same mathematical root**: matching distributions through a 1-Lipschitz potential (which fixes $\lVert\nabla D\rVert \le 1$, losing distance) paired with a *single global* Euclidean screening radius $`r_{\text{screen}} = \ell\sqrt{(c_{\text{screen}}+1)^2 - 1}`$ ($`3.873\,\ell`$–$`5.408\,\ell`$ at $\rho = 3.0$).
   - When an intra-mode pair $`(y_j, y_{j'})`$ inside a broad component ($\sigma_3 = 8\sigma_0$) exceeds $`r_{\text{screen}}`$ (because $\ell$ is pinned by the narrow high-mass component $\sigma_0$), it is screened into slack in **the exact same equation** (`c_hat > c_screen`) as a cross-valley orphan pair separated by $42.4\sigma$.
   - That is why Pillars 1 and 2 **inevitably couple**: expanding $\ell_Y(u)$ locally (Pillar 1) shifts the screening boundary for orphan detection (Pillar 2), and firing macro-transport on screened pairs (Pillar 2) destroys broad/anisotropic modes unless Pillar 1's scale-invariant $`r_{\text{loc}}`$ is embedded inside Pillar 2 (`_unified_v11/loss.py:169-175`). The two cases differ only in **topological separation** (overlapping/broad intra-mode support with $`r_{\text{loc}} \approx 1`$ vs empty-valley orphan support with $`r_{\text{loc}} \gg 1`$), NOT in separate mathematical laws.
2. **Law 3B — Conditional Fiber Drift–Variance Deconfounding & Sparse-Stratum Sample Complexity (Pillar 3):**
   Pillar 3 is **genuinely mathematically distinct** from Law 3A. Even if Law 3A is solved completely by an unconstrained $W_2$ Brenier oracle or exact conformal OT on every minibatch, conditioning on $(c, t)$ introduces two orthogonal statistical obstructions: (i) $A V_w A^\top$ base-drift leakage into fiber covariance under unresidualized context smoothing, and (ii) $\alpha B / T \lesssim 1$ finite-batch tail leverage starvation requiring cross-batch sufficient statistics or paired-context memory.

### 4.2 Exact Algebraic Audit of Midpoint Cancellation (Theorem 4)

[`docs/four-laws.md:150`](../../docs/four-laws.md#L150) and [`r3-conditional-ddgan.md:58-60`](../forge/studies/r3-conditional-ddgan.md#L58-L60) claim the exact midpoint cancellation identity $\mathbb{E}[r_X r_X^\top - r_Y r_Y^\top \mid c] = \Sigma_X(c) - \Sigma_Y(c)$. We prove the exact general identity and every condition under which it breaks:

> **Theorem 4 (Generalized Midpoint Cancellation & Finite-Sample Hat-Matrix Law):**
> Let $(X, Y)$ be paired at the **same** context $c$ with conditional means $m_X = \mathbb{E}[X \mid c]$, $m_Y = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid c]$ and covariances $\Sigma_X = \mathrm{Cov}(X \mid c)$, $\Sigma_Y = \mathrm{Cov}(Y \mid c)$. Define $\Delta := m_X - m_Y$ and $`m_{\text{mid}} := \frac{1}{2}(m_X + m_Y)`$.
> 1. **Deterministic centering $m(c)$ with midpoint error $`\delta_m := m_{\text{mid}} - m`$:** Setting $r_X := X - m$ and $r_Y := Y - m$, $`\mathbb{E}\!\left[r_X r_X^\top - r_Y r_Y^\top \mid c\right] = (\Sigma_X - \Sigma_Y) + \delta_m \Delta^\top + \Delta \delta_m^\top`$.
>    *Proof:* Write $m_X - m = \delta_m + \frac{1}{2}\Delta$ and $m_Y - m = \delta_m - \frac{1}{2}\Delta$. Expanding $\mathbb{E}[r_X r_X^\top \mid c] = \Sigma_X + (\delta_m + \frac{1}{2}\Delta)(\delta_m + \frac{1}{2}\Delta)^\top$ and subtracting $\mathbb{E}[r_Y r_Y^\top \mid c] = \Sigma_Y + (\delta_m - \frac{1}{2}\Delta)(\delta_m - \frac{1}{2}\Delta)^\top$, both $\delta_m \delta_m^\top$ and $\frac{1}{4}\Delta\Delta^\top$ cancel identically, leaving $\delta_m \Delta^\top + \Delta \delta_m^\top$. $\square$
> 2. **Exact breakdown under unequal weights:** For $`m_\alpha = \alpha m_X + (1 - \alpha) m_Y`$, $\delta_m = (\tfrac{1}{2} - \alpha)\Delta$, yielding exact bias **$`(1 - 2\alpha)\Delta\Delta^\top`$** (zero **iff** $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$ or $\Delta = 0$).
> 3. **Exact breakdown under basis misspecification, Tikhonov ridge, or EMA lag:** Whenever $`\hat{m}_{\text{pool}}(c)`$ shrinks nonlinear kinks via ridge $\Lambda$, misses basis terms outside $\mathrm{span}(\phi)$, or lags a moving generator $`G_\theta`$ across the FIFO ring, $\delta_m(c) \neq 0$, injecting rank-$`\le 2`$ bias $\delta_m \Delta^\top + \Delta \delta_m^\top$ whenever $\Delta(c) \neq 0$ (which vanishes automatically as $\Delta \to 0$).
> 4. **Exact finite-sample in-sample hat-matrix coupling:** Suppose $`(X_i, Y_i)_{i=1}^n`$ are independent across $i$ with conditional noise $`\varepsilon_{X,i} \perp \varepsilon_{Y,i}`$ and $`\hat{m}_{\text{pool}} = \frac{1}{2} H (X + Y)`$ for **any** linear smoother $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ (no symmetry required — this arises in [`particlegan/_cond_fiber/bank.py:360-420`](../../particlegan/_cond_fiber/bank.py#L360-L420) (`L413-419`, where `b_pool` centers `ring_x`/`ring_y`) and [`L554-597`](../../particlegan/_cond_fiber/bank.py#L554-L597), because past FIFO ring entries were already accumulated into `g_px`/`g_py` with non-symmetric EMA weights!). Writing $`\hat{m}_j = \bar{m}_j + \frac{1}{2}\sum_k h_{jk}(\varepsilon_{X,k} + \varepsilon_{Y,k})`$ with deterministic bias $`\delta_{m,j} = m_{\text{mid},j} - \bar{m}_j`$, the random smoother variance $`\frac{1}{4}\sum_k h_{jk}^2(\Sigma_{X,k} + \Sigma_{Y,k})`$ is identical in $`\mathbb{E}[r_{X,j} r_{X,j}^\top]`$ and $`\mathbb{E}[r_{Y,j} r_{Y,j}^\top]`$ and **cancels identically**, while the cross-term $`-h_{jj}\Sigma_{X,j}`$ minus $`-h_{jj}\Sigma_{Y,j}`$ leaves $`\mathbb{E}\!\left[r_{X,j} r_{X,j}^\top - r_{Y,j} r_{Y,j}^\top \mid C\right] = (1 - h_{jj})(\Sigma_{X,j} - \Sigma_{Y,j}) + \delta_{m,j}\Delta_j^\top + \Delta_j \delta_{m,j}^\top`$. Thus, even with in-sample ring reuse, **equilibrium bias at $\Sigma_X = \Sigma_Y$ and $\delta_m = 0$ remains strictly zero**, while off-equilibrium covariance signal attenuates by $`(1 - h_{jj})`$ (strongest at high-leverage outer-tail points!).
> 5. **Breakdown under unpaired contexts:** If $X$ is evaluated at $c_i$ and $Y$ at $c_j \neq c_i$, then $m_X(c_i) - m(c_i) \neq -(m_Y(c_j) - m(c_j))$ and $\frac{1}{4}\Delta\Delta^\top$ fails to cancel.
